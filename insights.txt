# insights
- Anticipation: Hear what's next.  Play M3 -> sus2 -> ... then what?  What do you hear?  Do that.  **cough** "voice-leading" **cough**
- Interval pivots: Play M3.  Don't move it.  From it, play maj -> min -> aug -> maj7 (M3 on bottom) -> maj7 (M3 on top) -> ... etc.
- Some intervals resolve to other intervals: P4 -> M3 (as in sus4), M2 -> M3 (as in sus2) ...
- More generally, all intervals happily change to other intervals.
- Depending on on how you switch intervals, it may imply a chord.  
    - M3 (1-3) -> P5 (1-5), can imply maj
    - M3 (1-3) -> M7 (1-7), can imply Maj7

GENERAL PRINCIPLE

White-key scales mostly organize into:
  RH  group of 3 + group of 4
  LH  group of 4/5 + group of 3

Thumb (1) normally passes UNDER.
Finger 3 or 4 normally passes OVER the thumb.

Avoid putting the thumb on a black key when a normal scale
fingering can avoid it.

CHORD COLOR / IDENTITY CHEAT SHEET
==================================

GENERAL TERTIAN RULE
root = tonal anchor
3/b3 = major/minor identity
7/b7 = functional family
5 = usually expendable unless altered
9/11/13 = color
b9/#9/#11/b13/#5/b5 = strong identity/color
For extended chords: preserve 3 + 7 + characteristic alteration/color.

BASIC TRIADS
maj        1 3 5              3 = major identity; 5 mostly reinforcement
min        1 b3 5             b3 = minor identity
dim        1 b3 b5            b5 = diminished instability; b3 = minor quality
aug        1 3 #5             #5 = augmented/whole-tone color
5          1 5                no 3 = major/minor ambiguity
sus2       1 2 5              2 + absent 3 = open suspended color
sus4       1 4 5              4 + absent 3 = suspended tendency; 4 often →3

6TH CHORDS
6          1 3 5 6            3 = major; 6 = warm tonic color
m6         1 b3 5 6           b3 = minor; 6 = strong Dorian implication
6/9        1 3 5 6 9          6+9 = consonant/open/pentatonic-ish color
m6/9       1 b3 5 6 9         b3+6 = Dorian; 9 = openness

7TH CHORDS
maj7       1 3 5 7            3+7 = major-major identity; 7 = defining color
7          1 3 5 b7           3+b7 = dominant tritone/function; 5 expendable
m7         1 b3 5 b7          b3+b7 = minor-7/modal identity
mMaj7      1 b3 5 7           b3+7 = defining minor/major contradiction
m7b5       1 b3 b5 b7         b5 = half-dim identity; b7 distinguishes from dim7
dim7       1 b3 b5 bb7        symmetrical m3 stack more important than root
maj7#5     1 3 #5 7           #5+7 = augmented-major color
7#5        1 3 #5 b7          3+b7 = dominant; #5 = altered/whole-tone color

9TH CHORDS
maj9       1 3 5 7 9          3+7 = identity; 9 = principal color
m9         1 b3 5 b7 9        b3+b7 = identity; 9 = lush/open minor color
9          1 3 5 b7 9         3+b7 = dominant; 9 = diatonic extension
7b9        1 3 5 b7 b9        b9 = intense root-semitone tension; harmonic-minor/Phrygian flavor
7#9        1 3 5 b7 #9        #9 = major/minor collision; blues/altered flavor

11TH CHORDS
m11        1 b3 5 b7 9 11     b3+b7 = identity; 11 = primary color; 9 secondary
11         1 3 5 b7 9 11      3 vs 11 = harsh m9; often omit 3 → dominant-sus sound
9sus4      1 4 5 b7 9         4 + absent 3 = suspended dominant identity
maj11      1 3 5 7 9 11       natural 11 clashes strongly with 3
maj9#11    1 3 5 7 9 #11      #11 = Lydian fingerprint; 3+7 = maj7 identity
9#11       1 3 5 b7 9 #11     3+b7 = dominant; #11 = Lydian-dominant/tritone color

13TH CHORDS
maj13      1 3 5 7 9 11 13    3+7 identity; 13 = upper color; often #11 instead of 11
13         1 3 5 b7 9 11 13   3+b7 = dominant; 13 = principal color
m13        1 b3 5 b7 9 11 13  b3+b7 = minor7; natural 13 strongly Dorian
7b13       1 3 5 b7 9 b13     b13 = dark altered/Aeolian-minor color
7#5        1 3 #5 b7           #5 and b13 same pitch class often; spelling implies chord tone vs extension

ALTERED DOMINANT CORE
ESSENTIAL: 3 + b7
ADD:
b9  = Phrygian/dark/semitone pressure
#9  = major/minor clash/blues
#11 = tritone/Lydian-dominant brightness
b13 = dark altered color
Example C7alt: E=3, Bb=b7, Db=b9, D#=#9, F#=#11, Ab=b13.
Perfect 5 generally unnecessary.

ADDED-NOTE CHORDS
add9       1 3 5 9            9 = color; no b7, therefore NOT dominant 9
m(add9)    1 b3 5 9           b3 = minor; 9 = strong expressive color
add11      1 3 5 11           3+11 = characteristic semitone/m9 rub
add#11     1 3 5 #11          #11 = immediate Lydian coloration
addb6      1 3 5 b6           5+b6 = exposed semitone; dark/modal
m(add6)    1 b3 5 6           natural 6 = Dorian implication
maj+b7     1 3 5 b7           b7 changes family toward dominant/Mixolydian

MODE-DEFINING COLOR NOTES
major + 4      = Ionian/plain major implication
major + #4     = Lydian
major + b7     = Mixolydian/dominant
minor + 2      = ordinary minor openness
minor + b2     = Phrygian
minor + 6      = Dorian
minor + b6     = Aeolian
minor + b5     = Locrian/diminished
dominant + #4  = Lydian dominant
dominant + b9  = Phrygian-dominant/harmonic-minor tendency
dominant + #5  = whole-tone/altered tendency

PITCH-COLOR DICTIONARY
b2/b9  = semitone pressure, Phrygian, altered dominant
2/9    = openness, spaciousness, diatonic extension
#2/#9  = blues, altered dominant, major/minor collision
b3     = minor identity
3      = major identity
b3+3   = split-third/blues/major-minor duality
4/11   = suspension; with 3 = deliberate rub
#4/#11 = Lydian/tritone/bright modern color
b5     = diminished/Locrian/altered
5      = stability, low information unless context depends on it
#5     = augmented/whole-tone/altered
b6/b13 = Aeolian/dark/altered
6/13   = warm major color; with minor = Dorian
b7     = dominant/Mixolydian/minor-7 world
7      = major-7/leading-tone color

SPLIT-THIRD
1 b3 3 5
b3+3 IS the identity.
Implications: blues, modal mixture, bitonality, chromatic voice leading.
Do not force it into exclusively major or minor.

AUGMENTED-SIXTH FAMILY
It+6   b6 1 #4          b6+#4 = defining augmented 6th; both tend →5
Fr+6   b6 1 2 #4        b6+#4 identity; 2 adds whole-tone-ish color
Ger+6  b6 1 b3 #4       b6+#4 identity; b3 gives dominant-7-like richness
Core principle: outward resolution b6→5 and #4→5 matters more than chord-root labeling.

QUARTAL HARMONY — PERSICHETTI
Do NOT search for a 3rd as primary identifier.
Identity = interval architecture/spacing.

P4+P4      e.g. C-F-Bb       pure quartal; open/pentatonic/root-ambiguous
P4+A4      e.g. C-F-B        upper tritone adds sharp tension
A4+P4      e.g. C-F#-B       lower tritone; often Lydian-ish if bass emphasized
P4 stacks  C-F-Bb-Eb-Ab       chain continuity = identity
A4 content                  increases tension/color sharply
Important variables:
1 interval sequence
2 P4 vs A4 placement
3 bass
4 top note
5 spacing/register
6 persistence of quartal grouping
If revoiced enough, same pitches may sound tertian instead.

QUINTAL HARMONY
e.g. C-G-D-A-E
identity = stacked 5ths
color = open/resonant/spacious/root-ambiguous
spacing is part of harmonic identity.

SECUNDAL HARMONY — PERSICHETTI
Identity = arrangement of M2/m2 rather than 3rds.
M2+M2       C-D-E          open/whole-tone or diatonic fragment
m2+m2       C-Db-D         maximum local semitone density
M2+m2       C-D-Eb         asymmetric; location of m2 gives directional tension
m2+M2       C-Db-Eb        same principle with different emphasis
Important:
1 collection
2 M2/m2 pattern
3 bass/top
4 register
5 density

CLUSTERS
Diatonic cluster   C-D-E-F-G       color = scale collection + density
Chromatic cluster  C-Db-D-Eb-E     color = semitone saturation
Whole-tone cluster C-D-E-F#-G#-A# color = M2 symmetry/root ambiguity
For large clusters prioritize:
1 pitch collection/set
2 register
3 m2 density
4 bass
5 top note
6 orchestration/dynamics
7 interior pitches
As saturation increases, individual note-function matters less.

POLYCHORDS — PERSICHETTI
Think TWO ways:
A) preserve separate chord units
B) translate upper chord against lower root as tensions

Analyze:
lower chord + upper chord + root interval + common tones + collisions + register.

Example D/C:
lower C major = C E G
upper D major = D F# A
relative to C: 9 #11 13
Can be heard either as C(9,#11,13) OR C-major + D-major polychord.
Register/grouping determines which reading wins.

USEFUL UPPER STRUCTURES OVER C7
D major   D F# A    = 9 #11 13       bright/Lydian-dominant
Eb major  Eb G Bb   = #9 5 b7        #9 dominant color
Ab major  Ab C Eb   = b13 1 #9       dark altered dominant
A major   A C# E    = 13 b9 3        b9+13 dominant color
Gb major  Gb Bb Db  = #11 b7 b9      highly altered
Bb minor  Bb Db F   = b7 b9 11       dark suspended/altered color

POLYCHORD PERCEPTION
common tones     = fuse units
semitones        = differentiate/intensify units
tritones         = differentiate/intensify units
wide register    = preserves separate chord identity
close register   = tends to fuse into one compound sonority

COMPOUND HARMONY — PERSICHETTI
Possible fusion:
tertian + quartal
tertian + secundal
quartal + cluster
multiple interval systems simultaneously
Do not insist on one root-extension label.
Prioritize:
1 bass
2 recognizable component structures
3 interval fingerprint
4 semitone/tritone collisions
5 top voice
6 register
7 density

MIRROR HARMONY — PERSICHETTI
Identity = intervallic reflection around an axis.
Important:
axis of reflection
interval pairs
resulting lower sonority
outer voices
symmetry
Generating/axis tone need NOT sound like harmonic root.

LARGE TERTIAN AGGREGATES — PERSICHETTI
Stacked beyond 7/9/11/13 into larger 3rd-built masses.
As size increases, prioritize:
bass
3rd quality
7th quality
altered intervals
top note
spacing/register
density
Do not overvalue extremely large chord symbols if ear perceives aggregate instead.

12-NOTE CHORDS
All pitch classes present → no unique "color pitch."
Harmony shifts toward:
spacing
register
bass
top voice
doubling
interval grouping
orchestration
dynamics
Rule: as pitch-class saturation approaches 12, vertical arrangement matters more than pitch inventory.

WHAT CAN USUALLY BE OMITTED?
root: omit if bass/instrument/context supplies it
perfect 5: usually first omission
9: omit unless 9 is desired color
11: retain if defining sus/minor/quartal color
13: retain if chord is specifically heard as 13
3: rarely omit unless sus/ambiguous/quartal structure intended
7: rarely omit from 7th/extended functional harmony
alterations: generally retain; often the entire reason for the chord

FAST ANALYSIS ALGORITHM
1. Is the chord tertian, quartal, secundal, cluster, polychordal, compound, symmetric?
2. If tertian: find 3/b3.
3. Find 7/b7 if present.
4. Find altered 5 or special interval.
5. Identify ONE note most responsible for extra color: b9/#9/9/11/#11/13/b13/etc.
6. Ask which notes can disappear without changing the perceived chord.
7. For non-tertian chords, analyze interval pattern before root/function.
8. For dense chords, inspect bass + top note + register before naming every interior pitch.

DEEPEST REDUCTION
IDENTITY = what makes the chord belong to a family.
COLOR    = what distinguishes this member of that family.
TENDENCY = what creates directional voice-leading pressure.

Examples:
C7           E+Bb = identity
C7b9         E+Bb = identity; Db = color/tendency
Cmaj7#11     E+B = identity; F# = color
Cm13         Eb+Bb = identity; A = Dorian color
C-Eb-E-G     Eb+E = defining split-third collision
C-F-Bb-Eb    fourth-chain = identity; no single "color tone" required
C-D-Eb-F#    M2/m2/tritone architecture may matter more than root label
